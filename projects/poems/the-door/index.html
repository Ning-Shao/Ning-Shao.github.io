The Door

Upon the park’s lone bench,
a philosopher lost in thought;
Across the street, the silent door,
her everlasting subject sought.

She is a traveler through time,
this is the home she has lost;
Her journeys misplaced the key,
the years have stolen her right to knock.

Every day she passes that door,
lifts her hand, then lets it fall;
Through the imagined slit of curtain,
she dreams the world concealed within.

She may not part, nor cease to muse,
until time grows thin,
and binds her soul within the shade,
where thought, once living, still remains.

门前

她坐在公园的木椅上，
像在思索的哲学家。
对街上紧闭的大门，
是她永恒的课题。

她是时间的旅人，
这是她遗失的家。
岁月剥夺了她的敲门权，
旅行弄丢了她的钥匙。

每日经过那扇门，
手举起，又放下。
透过那不存在的窗帘缝，
她猜想房子里面的世界。

她无法离开此地，
她会一直思索，
直到岁月将她化作幽魂，
成为这房子的影子一部分。

— Ning Shao

Author’s Note

I wrote The Door after watching Person of Interest. Harold Finch was once the “she” in the poem, the one who sits across the street from Grace’s house, passing her door countless times. He lost his right to knock because his creation, the Machine, placed him and those he loved in danger. He pondered so long that he almost became a ghost. Yet as I revised this story, I realized the poem also mirrors the relation between thought and being, between the human and the eternal, which generations have ceaselessly sought to reach.
